Surowiecki names four conditions for crowd wisdom: diversity of opinion, independence, decentralization, and a way to aggregate. Most agent swarms, our own first experiment included, meet only the last. Every agent sees the same input and brings the same pretraining, so nothing is decentralized.

Decentralization is the interesting one. In a human crowd each member holds a private signal. LLM agents have none unless someone gives it to them. So the thesis of the second paper is that LLM crowd wisdom has to be built. It comes from structuring what each agent knows, how it reasons and how the swarm combines views.

Intersecting knowledge

The flagship experiment takes a fraud case and splits its evidence into pieces: headers, body text, links, sender history, transaction metadata. Each agent gets a subset. We then vary the overlap between agents' subsets from fully disjoint to identical.

Our hypothesis is an inverted U. With too little overlap no agent can judge alone or cross-check another. With too much, the agents are redundant, which is where most swarms sit today. The best overlap is a design parameter nobody has estimated.

Li, Naito and Shirado showed at ICML 2026 that multi-agent LLMs reproduce a classic human failure: they converge early on shared evidence and leave distributed facts unexplored. We build on that. What we add is a continuous overlap sweep, a comparison between agents that deliberate and agents that only vote, and a matched human group.

Local knowledge

The second study uses frauds that are specific to Brazil, such as the Pix scam, the fake boleto and the “new phone number” message on WhatsApp, mixed with generic international phishing. We expect Portuguese-native and English-dominant models to fail on different items, and a mixed crowd to beat either alone.
