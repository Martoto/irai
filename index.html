[01] // THE CEILING

Past a point, more agents add nothing

When agents share their mistakes, what the crowd is worth levels off. At an error correlation of 0.25 the ceiling is four independent judges, whether you run fifty agents or fifty thousand.

We first ran into this in our own experiment. One LLM agent outscored every person in the room, and the crowd of people still outscored every crowd of agents. Other groups report the same limit in model ensembles and LLM judge panels.

Salles & Graeml, Echoes of Humanity (AMCIS 2026). Lefort et al. (2024). Abels & Lenaerts (IJCAI 2025). Kohli (2026, preprint).

[03] // DIVERSITY LADDER

Crowd wisdom has to be built

People in a crowd each know something the others don't. Agents built on one model all know the same things.

Here are eight ways to change that, shallowest first. Our research ranks them by how much each one lowers error correlation per dollar.

shallow▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█deep
  1. rung 0▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Sampling where most swarms stop

    Temperature and seeds. Noise around one shared belief.

  2. rung 1▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Persona

    Expert roles and demographic prompts. Cheap, and prone to caricature.

  3. rung 2▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Reasoning strategy

    Base rates, checklists, counterfactuals, red-team framing.

  4. rung 3▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Lineage

    Different providers, corpora and languages. Sabiá beside GPT beside Gemini.

  5. rung 4▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Information partition

    Each agent sees a different slice of the evidence.

  6. rung 5▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Tools and retrieval

    Different search engines, databases or document sets per agent.

  7. rung 6▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Weights

    Adapters fine-tuned on different data.

  8. rung 7▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█

    Human-seeded

    Agents conditioned on real people's interviews or answers.

We're testing a ninth rung, activation steering, which shifts how a model reasons without retraining it. The open question is whether it buys as much independence as a second lineage, and at what cost.

REC.01 // AUDIT
Neff Audit
Open for pilot engagementsBook a swarm audit

      

        
You run many agents because many opinions should beat one. The audit tells you how many opinions you're paying for and how many you're getting.

        

          
What the report contains

          

            
Error correlation
Pairwise correlation of your agents' errors, within and across models, prompts and tools.

            
Effective crowd size
The number of independent judges your swarm is worth.
Neff = N / (1 + (N − 1)ρ)

            
Diversity decomposition
How much of the crowd's gain comes from member competence and how much from their disagreement.

            
Cost and energy per decision
Tokens, money and estimated energy for each answer, against a single-call baseline.

          

        

        

          
How an audit runs

          
    
            
  1. Share outputsAgent or judge outputs on a labelled task set. We don't need your prompts or weights.
    2. 
            
  2. MeasureCorrelation matrix, effective size, discrimination and calibration.
    3. 
            
  3. PrescribeThe cheapest lever that adds independence: a second lineage, partitioned evidence, a different aggregation rule.
    4. 
            
  4. Measure againThe same metrics after the change, so the gain is on paper.

REC.02 // SIMULATION
Model Society
In research validationAsk about Model Society

      

        
A synthetic population of Brazil that starts from the real electorate and consumer base, and is scored against outcomes anyone can check. Latin America comes next.

        

          
The population is real data
Registered voters by section from the TSE, joined to IBGE Census 2022. The models never invent who lives where.

          
Models supply one quantity
The probability that a given kind of person makes a given choice. Turnout and population counts stay empirical, so every error can be traced to its source.

          
Granularity comes free
Post-stratification carries those probabilities to any level where the population mix is known: state, municipality, polling place, voting section.

          
Scored against its noise floor
Small units are noisy by nature. We report skill above a uniform-swing baseline at every level and say where it peaks.

          
Many lineages, measured
Portuguese-native models such as Sabiá and Lumi run beside global ones. We report where their errors cancel and where they share blind spots.

          
Local knowledge is tested
Before trusting what a model says about a place, we probe what it knows about that place and shrink the rest to zero.

        

        
Model Society outputs are synthetic simulations, never electoral polls. We release nothing about an election before polls close, and we don't publish synthetic voter quotes or portraits of small identifiable communities.

      

    

  


  

    

      
REC.03 // MONITORING
Guarda
In developmentAsk about Guarda

      

        
Most monitoring watches one agent and its permissions. Guarda watches what a group of agents does together. It's named for the guard bees that hover at a stingless-bee nest entrance.

        

          
Correlated failure
Alerts when agents start failing on the same items at the same time. That usually means a shared blind spot or a shared attack.

          
Collusion signals
Coordination between agents that the task didn't ask for, tracked as a property of the swarm.

          
Monoculture exposure
How much of your swarm one prompt injection could flip, by lineage.

          
Robust aggregation
Trimmed and Byzantine-robust rules in place of a plain median, so a compromised lineage can't carry the vote.

Research

    
We study how the collective intelligence of human crowds does and doesn't emerge in swarms of language models, and what it takes to build it on purpose.

  


  

    

      
[R.1] // OPEN QUESTIONS
Open questions we're working on

      

        
The diversity ladder, priced
Rank sampling, persona, reasoning strategy, lineage, information partition, tools and weights on one task, in error-correlation terms, per dollar.

        
Steering as a new rung
Where activation steering sits between a persona prompt and a fine-tuned adapter.

        
The overlap sweep
How much evidence agents should share. Too little and nobody can cross-check; too much and they're redundant.

        
Monoculture and collusion
Correlated attacks and emergent coordination as measurable swarm properties.

        
Local knowledge
A Local Knowledge Index per place and model, and a skill-by-scale curve from state down to voting section.

        
Hybrid collectives
When people and agents make different kinds of errors, and how to pool them.

        
Energy as a first-class metric
Accuracy per joule, against the single-call baseline.

      

    

  


  

    

      
[R.2] // INSTRUMENTS
The measurement kit
Every study and every audit reports the same five things, so results can be compared across tasks.

      

        
Error correlation
The average pairwise correlation of agents' errors, written ρ, reported within and across each diversity lever.

        
Effective crowd size
How many independent judges a swarm of N correlated agents is worth.
Neff = N / (1 + (N − 1)ρ)

        
Diversity prediction theorem
Page's exact identity for numeric tasks. It splits a crowd's accuracy into competence and diversity with no modelling assumptions.
crowd error = mean individual error − diversity

        
Discrimination and calibration
AUC and Brier score. A thresholded F1 alone can't tell a cautious agent from a wrong one.

        
Cost and energy per decision
A swarm that needs hundreds of calls for one answer has to earn them.

Papers
Working titles are marked as such. Planned work lists its design, never results.

      

        
Published
Echoes of Humanity: The Collective Mind of LLM Agents
An exploratory pilot. People and LLM agents rate the same emails for fraud. The best individual is an agent and the best crowd is human. Sampling temperature alone doesn't give agents the disagreement a crowd needs.
Salles & GraemlAMCIS 2026 Proceedings, SIG AIAA, Paper 1719Journal extension in preparation

        
In design
Built, Not Sampled: Engineering Crowd Wisdom in LLM Agent Swarms through Intersecting Knowledge
Two studies. The first splits the evidence of a fraud case between agents and sweeps the overlap from disjoint to identical, expecting accuracy to peak in between. The second tests whether Portuguese-native and global models fail on different Brazilian scams.
Working title

        
In design
A Nation of Models: Can an LLM Society of Brazil Forecast Where Polls Cannot See?
Persona-based forecasting with post-stratification, aimed at municipal swing and runoff vote transfer in the 2026 general election, with baselines and hypotheses fixed before the outcome.
Working titleRead the design notes

        
Planned
Hybrid human and AI collectives: error complementarity
If people and agents make different kinds of mistakes, a mixed crowd should beat both.

        
Planned
Monoculture risk: correlated attacks on swarms
Prompt injections embedded in fraudulent emails, single-lineage swarms against mixed ones, and median against robust aggregation.

        
Planned
Steering against lineage
Does diversity induced by activation steering lower error correlation as much as adding a second model family, and what does each cost? This is our first company experiment.

        
Long-running
Forecasting against the Focus survey
A swarm forecasts Brazilian inflation, interest and exchange rates each week, benchmarked against the Banco Central's survey of professional forecasters. The questions are future-dated, so training data can't leak the answers.

      

    

  


  

    

      
[R.4] // PROTOCOL
Rules we hold ourselves to

      

        
Preregister before outcomes exist
Forecasts are hashed and timestamped before the result is known.

        
Score against the noise floor
Every level of granularity has an error no model can beat. We report skill above it, never against zero.

        
Write up null results
A faithful structure with no predictive gain is a finding, and we commit to publishing it in advance.

        
Model lean is instrument bias
Refusals, hedges and ideological lean are logged and reported as properties of the models, never as findings about people.

        
Simulations aren't polls
Every synthetic output is labelled as one. Nothing electoral leaves the lab before polls close.

        
Label what's unrefereed
Preprints are cited as preprints.

[A] // ORIGIN // NANNOTRIGONA TESTACEICORNIS

    
About IRAI

    
IRAI is a Brazilian research company that measures and engineers the collective intelligence of AI swarms.

  

  

    

      

        
[A.1] // THE NAME

        
Named after a bee that locks its door at night

        
The iraí, Nannotrigona testaceicornis, is a stingless bee a few millimetres long, black with grey hairs, at home in hollow bricks and stone walls across Brazilian cities. Its name is usually read from Tupi as “river of honey”.

        
Every evening the colony seals its wax entrance tube. Every morning it opens it again. Guards stay around the entrance through the day.

        
We liked three things about it. A colony is many small workers with different jobs, which is our thesis about swarms. The guards are our safety work. And the name ends in AI.

      

      

        
[A.2] // THE COMPANY

        
Where the company comes from

        
IRAI grows out of ICIA, a research programme on how the collective intelligence of human crowds emerges in swarms of language models. Its first paper, Echoes of Humanity, was presented at AMCIS 2026.

        
What that programme found is why the company exists. Run one model a thousand times and you still have one opinion.

        
What we don't do

        
We don't sell orchestration, and we won't tell you to add agents. We count the independent judges in the swarm you have, on whatever framework you run.
